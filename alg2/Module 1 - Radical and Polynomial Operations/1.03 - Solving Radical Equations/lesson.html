1.03 — Solving Radical Equations

Isolating Variables in Radical Expressions

To solve radical equations, we must isolate the radical and then raise both sides to the appropriate power.

Steps to Solve

1. Isolate the radical term on one side of the equation.

2. Raise both sides to the index of the radical (e.g., square both sides for square roots).

3. Solve the resulting equation.

4. Check for extraneous solutions by plugging back into the original equation.

Example: x+3=5

Square both sides: x+3=25, so x=22.
