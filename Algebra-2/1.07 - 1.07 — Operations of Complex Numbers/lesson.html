1.07 — Operations of Complex Numbers

Mastering addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of numbers containing the imaginary unit i

Adding & Subtracting Complex Numbers

A complex number is written in the standard form:

a + b i

Where a is the real part, and bi is the imaginary part (with i=-1).

The Golden Rule: Treat the imaginary unit i just like an x in algebra. Combine real parts together, and combine imaginary parts together.

Example (Addition):

Simplify: (4+5i)+(2-3i)

=(4+2)+(5i-3i) =6+2i

Example (Subtraction):

Simplify: (7-2i)-(3-8i) (Don't forget to distribute the negative sign!)

=7-2i-3+8i =(7-3)+(-2i+8i) =4+6i

Multiplying Complex Numbers

To multiply complex numbers, use the distributive property (FOIL) just like you would with binomials. However, you must always apply the ultimate rule of imaginary numbers:

i2 = - 1

Step-by-Step Multiplication Example:

Multiply: (3+2i)(5-i)

Step 1 (Distribute): 3(5)-3(i)+2i(5)-2i(i) Step 2 (Simplify terms): 15-3i+10i-2i2 Step 3 (Combine like terms): 15+7i-2i2 Step 4 (Substitute i2=-1): 15+7i-2(-1) Step 5 (Final math): 15+7i+2=17+7i

Dividing Complex Numbers

In algebra, we cannot leave an imaginary unit i in the denominator of a fraction (just like we don't leave radicals there). To clear it, we must multiply the top and bottom by the complex conjugate of the denominator.

To find the conjugate, simply change the sign of the imaginary part:

  • The conjugate of a+bi is a-bi
  • The conjugate of 1+2i is 1-2i

Division Example:

Simplify the fraction:

5 1+2i

Step 1: Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the bottom (1-2i):

5 1+2i · 1-2i 1-2i = 5(1-2i) (1+2i)(1-2i)

Step 2: Expand the top and bottom:

Top:5-10i Bottom:1-2i+2i-4i2=1-4i2

Step 3: Replace i2=-1 in the denominator:

Denominator = 1 - 4 (-1) = 1 + 4 = 5

Step 4: Bring the fraction back together and simplify:

5-10i 5 = 5 5 - 10i 5 = 1 - 2 i
