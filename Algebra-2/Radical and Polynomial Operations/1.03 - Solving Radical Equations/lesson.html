1.03 — Solving Quadratic Equations

Comprehensive Methods for Finding Roots

In this lesson, we explore how to find the roots of quadratic equations using various algebraic techniques.

Standard Form and Factoring

A quadratic equation is expressed in standard form as: ax2+bx+c=0.

To solve by factoring, we look for two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b, then apply the Zero Product Property.

Example: x2-5x+6=0 factors into (x-2)(x-3)=0, giving roots x=2 or x=3.

The Quadratic Formula

For equations that do not factor easily, we use the quadratic formula:

x=-b±b2-4ac2a

The discriminant, D=b2-4ac, determines the number of solutions:

  • D>0: Two real solutions.
  • D=0: One real solution.
  • D<0: No real solutions.
