1.06 — Complex Numbers

1. The Imaginary Unit

Sometimes, we encounter equations that have no real solutions, such as x2=-1.

To solve this, we define the imaginary unit i as -1.

2. Defining Complex Numbers

A complex number is written in the form a+bi, where:

• a is the real part.

• b is the imaginary part (coefficient of i).

Example: In 5-3i, the real part is 5 and the imaginary part is -3.

3. Operations: Addition & Subtraction

To add or subtract complex numbers, simply combine the real parts and the imaginary parts separately.

Example: (2+4i)+(1-i)=3+3i.

Just treat i like any other variable during addition and subtraction!

4. Powers of i

Remember these fundamental powers of i:

i1=i

i2=-1

i3=-i

i4=1
