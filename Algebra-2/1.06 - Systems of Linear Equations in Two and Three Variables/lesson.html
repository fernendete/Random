Stepping into 3D: Systems in Three Variables

When you add a third variable (z), you leave flat graphs behind. An equation like Ax + By + Cz = D represents a flat plane in a 3D space.

The solution to a 3D system is an ordered triple (x, y, z) where three different planes intersect at a single shared point.

Let's solve this 3D system example step-by-step:

{ (A)x+y+z=6 (B)2x-y+z=3 (C)x+2y-z=2

Step 1: Eliminate z using equations (A) and (C).

Add equation (A) and equation (C) directly since the z terms cancel each other out:

(x+y+z) + (x+2y-z) = 6+2 2x+3y = 8 (Equation D)

Step 2: Eliminate z using equations (B) and (C).

Add equation (B) and equation (C) directly as well:

(2x-y+z) + (x+2y-z) = 3+2 3x+y = 5 (Equation E)

Step 3: Solve the new 2D system (Equations D & E).

From Equation E, we can easily isolate y:

y=5-3x

Plug this into Equation D:

2x+3(5-3x)=8 2x+15-9x=8 -7x=-7→x=1

Now find y by substituting x back in:

y=5-3(1)→y=2

Step 4: Back-substitute to find z.

Use original equation (A) with our known values:

1+2+z=6→3+z=6→z=3

The final solution is the ordered triple point: (1, 2, 3)
