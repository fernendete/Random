1.06 — Systems of Linear Equations in Two and Three Variables

Solving setups with multiple variables using substitution, elimination, and 3D intersections

Systems in Two Variables (2D)

A system of linear equations in two variables consists of two lines. The solution is the exact point (x, y) where they intersect.

There are three possible outcomes for a 2D system:

  • One Solution: The lines cross at exactly one independent point.
  • No Solution: The lines are parallel and never touch (Inconsistent system).
  • Infinite Solutions: Both equations represent the exact same line (Dependent system).

Core Methods to Solve:

  1. Substitution: Isolate one variable in an equation and plug it into the other.
  2. Elimination: Multiply equations by scalars and add them together to cancel out a variable.

Solving a 2D System Example

Let's find the point of intersection for this system:

{ 2x+y=7 x-3y=-7

Step 1 (Substitution): Isolate y in the first equation → y = 7 - 2x

Step 2: Substitute into the second equation → x - 3(7 - 2x) = -7

Step 3: Distribute and simplify → x - 21 + 6x = -7 → 7x = 14 → x = 2

Step 4: Plug x back in to find y → y = 7 - 2(2) → y = 3

The solution is the coordinates point: (2, 3)

Stepping into 3D: Systems in Three Variables

When you add a third variable (z), you leave flat graphs behind. An equation like Ax + By + Cz = D represents a flat plane in a 3D space.

The solution to a 3D system is an ordered triple (x, y, z) where three different planes intersect at a single shared point.

The Strategy to Defeat 3D Systems:

  1. Pick a pair of equations and use elimination to drop one variable (e.g., get rid of z).
  2. Pick a different pair of equations and eliminate the same variable.
  3. Now you have a standard 2D system with two equations and two variables. Solve it!
  4. Back-substitute your answers to uncover the final hidden variable.
